Review findings

Gap analysis

The operating model and process overview reviewed against what they are meant to achieve: less scope creep, less non-billable work, more people per project, greater perceived client value, more client confidence, and more transparency.

Each finding names the gap, the consequence, and the fix. Where a fix is marked fixed & built in, it is already part of the process — reflected in the guide, the spec builder and the templates. This page is the record of why each change was made.

What was already right

Worth stating plainly, because a list of twenty-two gaps reads more negatively than the review actually was. The underlying model is sound and most of it needed no change.

  • The sequencing is correct, and the reasoning given for it is correct. Sitemap before outlines, outlines before wireframes, blocks before design, spec before build — each of those orderings prevents a specific and expensive class of rework.
  • Separating decision log from change log is a genuinely good distinction that many agencies never make.
  • Separating reusable block structure from project-specific styling is the right architectural call, and it is what makes the block library compound in value.
  • Tying approval to named artefacts rather than general agreement is exactly right.
  • The light / standard / full routes correctly anticipate that a single heavyweight process would be ignored on small projects.
  • The TOGAF alignment is not decoration — it is what makes the process legible to procurement at larger clients.

Nearly every finding below is about the commercial and governance edges rather than the delivery sequence: what happens before discovery, what happens when something changes, and what happens after launch.
